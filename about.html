About Happyish

About me

My journey began in electronics in 1985, then moved into technology, design and computing, including about 15 years in front-end web development. Along the way, I studied Applied Psychology and Computing at degree level and completed an MA in Sound Design for the Moving Image at Bournemouth Media School.

AI first caught my attention in 1995. My work with AI and bots began before ChatGPT, with the past four or five years spent exploring their practical uses.

After lockdown, getting back into work proved difficult. Volunteering and reaching out to different services brought me into contact with many helpful people, but often left me feeling passed from one place to another without moving forward.

During that time, I started AIGENCY Limited, registering the name in 2024 and building a website around AI integration, tooling and digital systems. By the time I was preparing to launch this year, there was already a lot of interest. I could see huge business and financial potential, but that isn’t everything in life.

What mattered most to me was helping people use technology to make everyday life a bit easier.

Lived experience of recovery has also shaped how I think about confidence, change and the practical steps involved in rebuilding a life. I’d describe myself as neurospicy, too. That shapes how I learn, organise my thoughts and approach problems.

Using AI to organise my own thoughts, learn, work through problems and build confidence helped me get moving again. It also made me think about what it could offer others who feel stuck, overwhelmed or out of the loop — including people who are neurodivergent, in recovery or simply unsure where to start.

Happyish grew out of that experience: knowing technology, knowing what it is to need help, and wanting to bring those things together.

“We keep what we have by giving it away.” That idea is part of my philosophy: sharing the knowledge, support and confidence that others have freely given me. It’s why I want Happyish to develop into a Community Interest Company. Building it is also part of my own reintegration — finding my way back into work through something I care about.

The aim is to make technology feel less intimidating, less complicated and more useful. Sometimes that means AI. Sometimes it means working through a form, writing an email, making a plan or learning a new skill. The starting point is always what the person is trying to do.

Happyish shouldn’t be another place that simply tells people where else to go. It should sit with the problem, help move it forward and leave someone more confident about taking the next step themselves.
